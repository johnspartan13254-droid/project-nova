A Long Arc of Learning, Creativity, and Systems Thinking

WakeForms is built on the idea that deep, structured learning and speculative visual systems can coexist as one continuous trajectory. This page connects a multi-year education plan with the marine-facing Reflective-Surface Harmonic Field (RSHF) framework, showing how both contribute to a unified creative and research ecosystem.

The Eight-Year Plan

The education plan is designed as a deliberate, multi-phase arc: building foundations, specializing, integrating systems, and ultimately developing a research-informed studio practice.

Years 1–2: Foundations

Focus on core undergraduate coursework, strengthening fundamentals in analysis, communication, and technical skills that support future systems thinking and visual experimentation.

Years 3–4: Transfer & Specialization

Transition into a program that supports interdisciplinary work, with emphasis on fields that intersect with optics, media, computation, and conceptual modeling.

Years 5–6: Systems Integration & Studio Development

Begin formalizing WakeForms as a creative/technical studio identity, integrating coursework with speculative frameworks like RSHF and building a portfolio of concept-driven projects.

Years 7–8: Master’s & Research Portfolio

Pursue graduate-level work that deepens research into visual systems, resonance-inspired models, and marine-facing concept portfolios, positioning WakeForms as a long-term ecosystem.

Why Deep-Sea Biology and Optics?

Deep-sea environments are defined by limited human visibility, unusual light behavior, bioluminescence, transparency, reflection, particle scattering, and unknown signal interpretation. RSHF uses those themes as conceptual inspiration rather than empirical explanation.
