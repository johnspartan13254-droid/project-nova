My Eight-Year Education Plan

Transparent. Intentional. Adaptive over time.

By Travis · Published August 2026

This page shows how I intend to move from part-time undergraduate study to a master's degree over an eight- to ten-year horizon while keeping the path financially realistic, publicly accountable, open to informed guidance, and free of private student loan debt.

It is also a public invitation for sponsors, private or individual investors, educators, collaborators, and observers to help test, strengthen, fund, critique, or redirect the plan where responsible opportunities emerge.

??? Overview

I'm sharing this publicly because anyone following this journey deserves the full map, not just the highlights.

This is my structured education path from part-time undergraduate study to a master's degree, including how I plan to finance it, where my income will come from, and how my own software architecture helps sustain the plan.

This page is written for people who can help evaluate, strengthen, challenge, sponsor, invest in, teach, fund, or contextualize the path: curriculum leaders, business heads, education professionals, finance partners, platform collaborators, private backers, individual supporters, and observers who see possibilities I may not yet see.

Eight to ten years is a long horizon, which is exactly why this plan cannot be treated as fixed. It needs to stay stable enough to navigate by while remaining responsive to funding changes, curriculum shifts, platform evolution, professional opportunities, sponsorship openings, and what becomes relevant to the people watching over time.

?? Academic Timeline

This timeline is a planning model, not a rigid promise. Each phase is meant to be reviewed and adjusted as transfer requirements, program availability, funding opportunities, work capacity, sponsorship potential, and real-world learning conditions change.

Years 1–2: Foundation Phase

I begin as a part-time student, enrolling in 6–9 credits per semester at the community college level. This pace is deliberate: it helps protect Pell Grant eligibility, supports income continuity, and gives me room to build strong academic habits before scaling up.

Focus: Core general education requirements, writing, and introductory coursework in my target field.

Years 3–4: Acceleration Phase

As my financial and operational systems stabilize, I increase to half-time enrollment and begin targeting transferable credits with intention, aligned to the requirements of a four-year institution.

Focus: Discipline-specific prerequisites, transfer articulation planning, and GPA protection.

Year 5: Transfer and Transition

Transfer to a regionally accredited four-year university. I enter as a junior with a clean transfer package and a clear degree pathway. Every credit before this point was placed with transfer in mind.

Years 6–7: Upper-Division Undergraduate

Full focus on completing my bachelor's degree. Pell Grant funding may be tapering by this phase, so income sourcing and institutional aid carry more weight. I target graduation with no private loan debt.

Goal: Bachelor's degree, conferred.

Year 8: Graduate Entry

Graduate Entry (Master's Program)

With an undergraduate credential and professional experience accumulated through WakeForms, I enter a master's program and pursue a graduate assistantship, employer tuition benefit, grant, fellowship, or graduate-specific aid.

Goal: Master's degree, conferred.

?? Pell Grant Pacing Strategy

The Federal Pell Grant has a lifetime eligibility cap equivalent to 12 full-time semesters. My strategy is to use that eligibility carefully, matching enrollment pace to financial stability instead of rushing through aid before the plan is ready to support itself.

Early years: Enroll part-time to minimize per-semester drawdown while I build financial and operational stability.

Mid-years: Scale to half-time, drawing the grant at 50% of the scheduled award per term — stretching eligibility across more semesters.

Later years: Accept that Pell eligibility may be largely consumed by upper-division, and shift reliance toward earned income, institutional scholarships, and assistantships.

Graduate phase: Pell Grant funding does not cover graduate study, so this phase is financed through work, assistantships, and grant or fellowship applications.

This is not a hack — it's responsible stewardship of a public resource I qualify for and intend to use exactly as intended.

?? Income-Sourcing Strategy

I am not taking on private student loan debt. That boundary shapes every financial decision in this plan, but it does not close the door to sponsorship, private investment, individual backing, negotiated support, or aligned funding structures that are transparent, voluntary, and responsibly documented.

The income sources across the plan are:

1. WakeForms

My companion architecture platform — a forms and workflow system I've designed and am developing — is my primary earned income engine. Revenue from subscriptions, consulting, and deployments funds my living expenses and any tuition gaps not covered by aid.

2. Freelance and Contract Work

Technical services I can deliver remotely and asynchronously, compatible with a student schedule.

3. Federal Aid

Pell first, always. If a subsidized federal loan becomes necessary, I borrow the minimum and document the repayment plan before borrowing.

4. Institutional Aid

I pursue merit and need-based scholarships at every institution I attend. This is a standing item in my annual planning cycle.

5. Sponsorships, Investment, and Aligned Partnerships

I am open to sponsorships, brand partnerships, platform support, private or individual investment, and aligned collaborations when they genuinely fit the education path, the content being created, and the community observing the work.

These are possible support channels, not guarantees or replacements for earned income, public aid, careful planning, or written terms.

The throughline: every dollar spent on tuition should be earned, qualified for, sponsored, invested through genuine alignment, or supported through transparent public or institutional aid. No private student loan debt, no hidden assumptions, and no closed door to ideas that can be responsibly tested and negotiated.

Hypothetical Investor Return Paths

Any return on investment would need to be discussed carefully and put in writing before anything is agreed to. Possible structures could include revenue sharing from WakeForms, a small equity position tied to the platform, credited sponsorship or public recognition, repayment from future earned income, or another negotiated structure that fits the amount, timing, risk, and shared goals of the relationship.

This is not an offer, solicitation, or promise of any specific return. It is an invitation to begin a responsible conversation. Terms, expectations, recognition, timing, and educational direction would need to be negotiated and documented, especially if an investor, sponsor, educator, or field expert can help nudge the plan toward a more valuable course of study, credential path, or applied project.

??? WakeForms: More Than a Side Project

WakeForms is my companion architecture — a system I've designed to handle structured data collection, routing, and workflow management in contexts that standard form tools don't serve well.

In the context of this education plan, WakeForms plays three roles:

Income Engine

It is an active, deployable product. Revenue from it directly subsidizes my tuition and living costs throughout this journey.

Professional Credentialing

Every year I spend building WakeForms is a year of applied, documented technical experience. By the time I enter a master's program, I arrive with a portfolio, not just a transcript.

Academic Integration

The problems WakeForms is designed to solve intersect with my academic interests. My coursework informs the product; the product grounds my coursework in real applications.

WakeForms is not a distraction from school. It is the reason I can afford to go.

Because the surrounding decade will change, WakeForms is also treated as an adaptive architecture rather than a finished object. Its role may expand, narrow, pivot, or connect with partners depending on what education, business, finance, and community use cases reveal over time.
